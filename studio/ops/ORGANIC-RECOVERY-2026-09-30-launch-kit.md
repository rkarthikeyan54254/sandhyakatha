# Three evenings of stories — launch kit

Prepared September 30, 2026. **Drafts for Rama's use/review; nothing sent or published.** Use existing approved stories for an English-first trial with parents of children aged 6–10. Ask the organiser's language preference before extending it. Existing Hindi/Tamil editions can be used after verifying their live pages; new native copy still needs human native-language review.

The offer: three evenings, one story each evening, about 6–7 minutes to read aloud, and one open question. Free, no app installation or sign-in needed to read the public story. This is an optional Hindu-story resource for interested parents, not a school curriculum or an endorsed institutional programme.

Night 2 retains the distinction between the Kiṣkindhākāṇḍa episode and Uttarakāṇḍa's curse tradition. Do not compress it into an assertion that a curse is explicitly lifted in the first passage. Night 3 carries a social-hierarchy parent note. Do not describe Guha as the person who rows the boat; the live story explicitly says otherwise.

## One reusable product-demo brief

Audience: a parent seeking a read-aloud Hindu story for a child aged 6–10. Format: a short screen demonstration with Rama's own narration if convenient. Asset: the already-approved Hanuman-tail page, no new story art or retelling required.

1. Open with the concrete use: “A story to read with your child tonight.” Show the title and six-minute label.
2. Briefly show the source note and tap a name for pronunciation help.
3. Show the question at the end: this is something to do together after reading.
4. End with one platform-appropriate action: Instagram/YouTube “Tap the first link on our profile”; Facebook “Open the story in this post.”

Caption draft: “Tonight: The Tail Bhīma Could Not Lift. A six-minute read-aloud story for ages 6+, with its Mahābhārata source and a question to discuss afterwards. [Platform-specific instruction and link.]”

Compare this demonstration against one existing teaser using correctly tagged site arrivals and voluntary read feedback. The audience is too small for claims of statistically proven creative superiority. Change one thing at a time and keep whichever earns relevant readers with less production effort.
