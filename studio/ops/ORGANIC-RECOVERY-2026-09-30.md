# Sandhya Katha: organic recovery through October 15

Prepared September 30, 2026. Constraint: no additional paid traffic; Rama is in Bangalore, has no direct temple contact, and can spend at most one hour a day when available. The earlier suggestion of warm access was clarified: plan for **cold outreach**, with no assumed partner audience.

## Decision

Keep 5,000 rolling-28-day active users as an ambition, but retire October 15 as the operating forecast. Use the next fortnight to establish one repeatable path: **a relevant parent community recommends one story → a parent reads on the site → voluntarily follows the existing WhatsApp channel → comes back for another evening → shares with one other parent**.

The immediate acquisition experiment is a three-evening parent read-aloud trial, ages 6–10, offered to small Bangalore reading communities. Give the organiser a complete, low-effort package and ask them to review it before sharing. Use existing approved stories. Lead with the parent benefit: a story chosen for tonight and a question to discuss. Source transparency supports trust after that promise.

## What 5,000 by October 15 would require

The target is 5,000 **distinct active users in September 18–October 15**, not 5,000 visits, views, families or readers. Only 815 of the currently measured users were active September 18–29 and are already inside that future window. The other 529 in the current 28-day total cannot be counted unless they return in the new window.

That leaves approximately **4,185 additional distinct qualifying users**, less any new qualifying September 30 users. Ignoring today's unknown contribution, October 1–15 would need about **279 additional unique users per day**. Returns by people already in the 815 support habit but do not close this unique-user gap. This is roughly 70 times the recent daily level, with no confirmed distribution partner.

Illustrative partner scenarios below are arithmetic, **not forecasts or benchmark conversion rates**. Reach must be relevant, unduplicated parents actually exposed to the invitation; do not use total followers as reach. The final 80% is an assumed share of visitors who qualify as GA4 active users and are incremental to the existing window.

| Scenario | Partners × distinct parent reach each × website visit share × incremental-active share | Added active users |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| No cold partner accepts | 0 | 0 from this route |
| Small trial works | 2 × 500 × 15% × 80% | 120 |
| Strong partner response | 4 × 1,000 × 20% × 80% | 640 |

At 15% visit share and 80% incremental-active share, the full gap requires about **34,875 distinct parent exposures**. No such reach is secured. Reforecast on October 7 from measured partner results; do not buy visits to preserve the deadline.

## The channel strategy

### 1. Partner recommendations: primary acquisition test

Start with the first three public leads in the [launch kit](ORGANIC-RECOVERY-2026-09-30-launch-kit.md). They already describe reading/storytelling work for families. Relevance is verified; willingness, response speed and reachable audience are unknown. Ask for an optional resource review and a single parent invitation. Avoid asking a cold contact to promote an unfamiliar platform every day or to export their parent list.

Send two individually adapted messages on each of three available days. Follow up once after three business days. If the first six produce no positive reply, spend the next outreach slot improving the offer and speaking to one organiser; do not compensate with bulk sends. Larger institutions are secondary because programme approval may outlast October 15.

For general libraries, clearly identify Sandhya Katha as **Hindu stories**, offered to interested families. Do not present it as a neutral curriculum for every family. For religious programmes, preserve the exact source and regional-tradition notes; partnership does not imply endorsement or licence to change the telling.

### 2. WhatsApp: repeat visits after the first useful story

Use the existing channel. One predictable evening update, with a short invitation and a direct story link. Ask families to follow voluntarily after trying the story. Partner groups receive only the posts their administrator agrees to; daily continuation belongs on the opted-in channel. No new group, CRM, newsletter system or phone-number collection is needed for the pilot.

The trial has three distinct nights: Hanuman-tail, Hanuman-reminded, Guha. Public English pages show 6, 6 and 7 minutes respectively, so the packet promises **about 6–7 minutes**, not an exact six every time. Age guidance and parent notes stay attached. The kit contains usable links and copy.

After the third night, ask the organiser for an aggregate count of parents who voluntarily report reading on at least two nights. This is self-reported trial retention, separate from GA4. Continue with three reliably delivered evenings a week if daily work is too much. A schedule that can be sustained is more valuable than an abandoned daily promise.

### 3. Instagram/Facebook: make a parent try the product

For the next two weeks, cap new production at two parent-facing demos or invitations per week. Reuse the approved story, show the actual story page, and use one primary instruction: read this story tonight. A 20–30-second demonstration can show the story, name help and the question at the end. Do not spend the daily hour creating three-language variants of every asset.

On Instagram, make the first profile link match the current featured story. Use a Story link sticker when available and verify it on a phone; the inspected caption URLs are not a verified clickable route. On Facebook, use a complete HTTPS story URL with Facebook tags. Cross-post the creative, but generate each platform's link separately.

Keep scheduled posts only after checking for duplication and matching destinations. Five YouTube Shorts are verified as scheduled through October 4; Facebook/Instagram future queues still need reconciliation. Avoid adding more posts blindly. The pilot should replace a production slot, not add another daily obligation.

### 4. YouTube: a limited conversion test, not the core forecast

Keep the existing Shorts queue. Test a clear spoken/on-screen invitation early enough to be seen: “Read this with your child tonight. Open our channel and tap the first link.” Point that first profile link to the featured story with clean YouTube tags. If several stories are promoted simultaneously, a clear existing story index is better than promising one story and landing on another; test one featured story first.

YouTube officially makes URLs in Shorts descriptions/comments non-clickable. Channel profile links are clickable. A related-video bridge is possible only through another YouTube video and with advanced-feature access; there are currently no public long-form videos and Studio showed a verification notice, so do not make that bridge a launch dependency. [YouTube link rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639?hl=en), [related video requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en).

Tamil Shorts have promising discovery signals, but publication date and exposure differ. Test two existing approved Tamil assets against their actual site arrivals before creating a large Tamil batch. Do not label 8,119 views versus seven `youtube/shorts` sessions a conversion rate: the link tags are cross-contaminated and profile visits may arrive as direct.

### 5. Search: protect and grow the existing foundation

The sitemap is already successful: 212 discovered URLs, read September 29. The indexing report is dated September 21: 13 indexed, 96 discovered/not indexed, one crawled/not indexed and one redirect error. Inspect a small set of priority pages and the historical Navaratri error using live URL Inspection. Do not rewrite redirects merely because of an old report.

Obtain relevant editorial links from willing partners to the actual story/resource they reviewed. Improve existing festival and source pages before making more SEO pages. Search has only two reported clicks, and Google makes no indexing/timing guarantee; use it for compounding discovery after the pilot. [Google sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).

## Success and the scorecard

October 15 operating targets, explicitly **targets rather than forecasts**: 1–2 willing distribution partners; 20–50 parents who voluntarily confirm trying a story; at least 10 reporting reading on two different nights; 30–50 voluntary channel followers; and a rising seven-day unpaid traffic baseline. Treat 20 measured unpaid active users/day in the final week as a stretch checkpoint, not a promise. A failed cold-outreach experiment with saved evidence is preferable to pretending reach equals readership.

For steady daily use, reason backwards from repeat readers: 100 willing parent readers × an assumed 20% visiting on a given evening = 20 visits that evening. The assumed 20% must be measured; it is not current retention. Acquisition replenishes this group, and the evening service gives them a reason to return.

Record on October 3, 7, 11 and 15, using complete elapsed days and the same windows:

| Metric | Definition / guardrail |
| --- | --- |
| Outreach | Approached / positive replies / agreed shares / actually shared, with dates; not one blended total |
| Acquisition | Campaign-level deduplicated active users and sessions by exact source; exclude partner-review links and owner QA where identifiable; keep direct/unknown separate |
| Engagement | Engaged sessions / sessions; this is not completed storytelling |
| App use | `story_opened` and `story_finished` user and event counts reported separately |
| Public-page use | Page visitors and engagement until validated public-reading instrumentation exists; do not invent finishes |
| Return use | Aggregate voluntary trial reports on at least two distinct nights; GA new/returning as a separate directional metric, not family retention |
| Channel growth | Current followers minus baseline 12, with observation date; not proof of reading |
| Efficiency | Minutes spent per route and confirmed trial readers; discontinue high-effort routes with no relevant response |

Do not compare platform impressions with GA users as if they share a denominator, sum daily active users into monthly uniques, or interpret missing/unsupported fields as zero. Preserve experiment links instead of changing UTMs halfway through.
