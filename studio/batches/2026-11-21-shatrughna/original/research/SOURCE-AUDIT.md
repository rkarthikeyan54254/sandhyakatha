# Shatrughna pair — source audit and editorial review notes

**Status:** in-review; not approved, not published. **Languages:** English, Tamil, Hindi. **Renditions:** independently composed short (~3 min target) and full (~6 min target), subject to read-aloud timing by a native-language reviewer.

## Text base

- Primary narrative locus: *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, **Uttara Kāṇḍa**, following received/vulgate chapter numbers used by Hari Prasad Shastri's English translation (1952), Book 7, chapters 60–71.
- Read and compared the accessible Sanskrit text of **7.66.1–14** (birth of twins) and **7.69** (battle with Lavaṇa) against Shastri's corresponding translation. For the battle's distinctive reply, 7.69.4 in the received numbering: **शत्रुघ्नो न तदा जातो यदान्ये निर्जितास्त्वया**. *“Śatrughna had not yet been born when you defeated the others.”* This is a **partial** translation of the verse (it continues with a challenge).
- **Baroda/Oriental Institute critical-edition page-by-page verification remains pending.** Do not mark cross-recension stability as verified. A source comparison page identifies the newborns' “young moon” visual simile as present in a popular text but not the critical edition; that image was deliberately omitted from all six versions of the second story.
- The **Uttara Kāṇḍa** is part of the received Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa. Its textual history and critical-edition numbering require a tradition note for publication. Do not treat received-text sarga numbers as critical-edition sarga numbers.

## Story A — The Warrior at the Gate

**Passages and facts** (Hari Prasad Shastri chapter numbering):

1. **7.60–61**: Sages approach Rāma; Madhu received Rudra's trident for his merits; his son Lavaṇa terrorises living beings.
2. **7.62**: Bharata volunteers. Śatrughna says Bharata has already borne hardship for fourteen years in Nandigrāma and volunteers instead.
3. **7.63**: Rāma installs Śatrughna, gives a divine arrow, and explains why Lavaṇa must be intercepted while his trident is inside the city. Chapter **7.64** also concerns preparation for the mission.
4. **7.67**: Cyavana recounts King Māndhātā's destruction by Lavaṇa's trident.
5. **7.68**: Śatrughna waits at Madhupura's gate, challenges Lavaṇa, and prevents him returning inside for his weapon.
6. **7.69**: Śatrughna's boast; trees hurled and shot apart; blow to the head; unconsciousness; Lavaṇa assumes him dead; Śatrughna recovers and uses divine arrow; trident returns to Rudra.
7. **7.70**: Śatrughna seeks to possess/govern the city and it prospers over twelve years.

**Interpretation separated from fact:** “He rose again to complete his duty” and “he volunteered to spare his brother” are story conclusions grounded in the sequence and dialogue, not quotations from the Sanskrit. No invented character, secret rescue, mysterious messenger or new weapon is introduced.

**Care note:** A rākṣasa kills living beings; a king and army are said to be reduced to ashes in a retrospective account; Śatrughna is knocked unconscious; Lavaṇa dies. The action is not gore-focused. Suggested age floor 8+, pending parental review.

## Story B — The Night Shatrughna Heard the Rāmāyaṇa

**Passages and facts:**

1. **7.65–66**: Śatrughna stays at Vālmīki's hermitage on the journey. On the **same night**, Sītā gives birth to **two sons**. Vālmīki performs rites using kuśa grass and names the elder **Kuśa**, the younger **Lava**. Śatrughna hears the news, goes to Sītā's hut and offers a congratulatory blessing. He leaves the next morning. Sanskrit **7.66.1–2, 11–14** corroborates the chronology.
2. **7.70**: Śatrughna governs a prosperous city for **twelve years** and wishes to see Rāma again.
3. **7.71**: On his return journey he visits Vālmīki; the sage praises his feat and blesses him. After a meal Śatrughna hears a musical account of Rāma's deeds, accompanied by stringed instruments. He is moved to tears, and companions are astonished. He discourages them from questioning Vālmīki out of curiosity.

**Critical editorial limitation:** Chapter 7.71 **does not identify the singers in that scene**; it does not say that Śatrughna knowingly listened to his nephews, recognized them or made a pact of silence. Do not add such claims, even though later chapters name Lava and Kuśa as singers elsewhere. The drama comes from the two visits and his response, not a fabricated revelation.

**Care note:** Sītā is living away from Ayodhyā under Vālmīki's protection; a child may ask why. The story includes childbirth and family separation, but no violence in its narrated events. Suggested age floor 7+, pending review.
