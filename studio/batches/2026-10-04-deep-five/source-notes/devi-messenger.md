# Devī and Śumbha's messenger — selection note

**Primary witness:** *Devī Māhātmya* (within the *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*), [chapter 5](https://devimahatmyam.aurobharati.in/devimahatmyam/episode-3/chapter-5/) and [chapter 6](https://devimahatmyam.aurobharati.in/devimahatmyam/episode-3/chapter-6/), Sanskrit with verse-by-verse English. [Pargiter's *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* translation, canto 85](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-markandeya-purana/d/doc117163.html) is a cross-check; its canto numbering differs from the *Devī Māhātmya* chapter numbering.

## Claim ledger

| Claim safe for narration | *Devī Māhātmya* verses |
| --- | --- |
| Śumbha and Niśumbha seize the gods' sovereignty; the gods remember Devī's promise and praise her in the Himalaya. | 5.2–82 |
| Pārvatī arrives at the Gaṅgā; a radiant form emerges from her body-sheath and is called Kauśikī. Pārvatī's remaining dark form is called Kālikā. | 5.84–88 |
| Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa report Devī's beauty to Śumbha, who sends Sugrīva as messenger. | 5.89–104 |
| Sugrīva boasts of Śumbha's power and possessions and presses Devī to choose Śumbha or Niśumbha. | 5.106–114 |
| Devī says she has promised to marry only one who defeats her in battle and matches her strength. The messenger belittles and threatens her, including a threat to drag her by the hair. | 5.116–126 |
| Devī maintains her promise and tells the messenger to report her words. Śumbha then sends Dhūmralocana to seize her; he charges, and she reduces him to ash with a single *huṃ*. | 5.128–129; 6.2–13 |

**Episode boundary and identity:** End at 6.13, the first attack's resolution, while acknowledging in source notes that the larger battle continues in 6.14–10. Do not import that later battle into this episode. Chapter 5.88 calls the dark form remaining after Kauśikī's emergence Kālikā; chapter 7 separately describes Kālī emerging from Devī's forehead. Do not collapse those descriptions into a single unsupported transformation. This is distinct from existing `durga-mahishasura` (chapters 2–3), `shakambhari`, and `kali-stops` (later Śumbha–Niśumbha war), but should cross-reference `kali-stops` without repeating its battle.

**3/6-minute feasibility:** Strong, subject to child read-aloud review. The short edition can cover the gods' loss, Kauśikī's emergence, Sugrīva's demand, Devī's answer, and Dhūmralocana's failed attack in roughly 300–350 spoken words. The full edition can add selected images from the gods' hymn, Śumbha's boast and the messenger exchange in roughly 620–690 words. Do not pad either edition with the later lion-and-army violence.

**Age and care:** Consider minimum age 9–10 and a parent note: the text includes a coercive marriage proposal, gendered belittlement, a hair-dragging threat, and a death in battle. State the threat accurately without making Devī a possession in the narrator's voice or dwelling on the violence. Her battle vow belongs to this sacred narrative; it is not advice that children should confront a threatening person alone. Avoid invented private motives or dialogue, and preserve the theological distinction among Pārvatī, Kauśikī and Kālikā in the source note even if the spoken story is simpler.
