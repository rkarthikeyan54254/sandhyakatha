# King Nṛga in the dry well — source-selection note

**Selection verdict:** strong candidate for independent 3- and 6-minute English tellings, pending editorial source review. No matching Nṛga/lizard canon row was found on 2026-10-04. Use *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.64 as the single narrative witness. The [chapter text with individual Sanskrit verses and English translation](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/64/) is the working primary-text locator; the [chapter in Tagare's translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-bhagavata-purana/d/doc1128942.html) is a potential independent edition check, but its page returned 403 in this audit and has **not** been collated. The VedaBase site's theological purports and chapter synopsis are interpretive layers, not extra narrated events.

**Non-import boundary:** Do not say the two brāhmaṇas *cursed* Nṛga: the chapter synopsis uses that word, but verses 16–24 narrate an unresolved ownership dispute followed by Yamarāja's choice, not a spoken curse. Do not invent a settled refund, a motive to steal, a negotiation with the cow, or a promise of permanent liberation. The narrated result is a heavenly form and departure by celestial vehicle. Do not turn Kṛṣṇa's later, specifically brāhmaṇa-property warning into a source claim that any unintentional paperwork error has this consequence. A later prose retelling such as *Kṛṣṇa Book* is not the selected witness.

**Length and child-facing care:** The well rescue, mistaken gift, competing claims, failed restitution, choice, lizard life, and Kṛṣṇa's rescue make a complete action sequence. A 300–360-word short version and a 650–680-word full version appear feasible without added incidents; the full can retain more of the cow and gift details, the two men's divergent refusals, and Nṛga's memory. That is a drafting judgment, not a measured duration. Recommend a parent-facing note and an initial **9+ editorial review**, not a fixed canon age yet. The chapter's remaining warning (10.64.31–43) invokes caste-specific property protections, multigenerational ruin and hell; 10.64.14–15 lists women among gifts. These passages should be disclosed to editors/parents, not smuggled into a generic “good deeds cancel mistakes” or “punishment was fair” bedtime moral. Keep the king's regret and both claimants' refusal legible while allowing a child to ask whether the result seems proportionate.
