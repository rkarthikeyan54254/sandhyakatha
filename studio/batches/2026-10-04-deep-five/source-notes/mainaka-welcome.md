# Hanumān and Maināka — selection note

**Witness:** *Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa*, Sundara Kāṇḍa [5.1.87–143](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/sundara/sarga1/sundaraitrans1.htm), Sanskrit with verse-by-verse translation; [continuous prose](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/sundara/sarga1/sundara_1_prose.htm) for a second reading.

## Claim ledger

| Claim safe for narration | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| During Hanumān's flight to find Sītā, Ocean decides to help Rāma's envoy and asks submerged Maināka to offer him rest. | 5.1.87–101 |
| Maināka rises with gold-coloured peaks; Hanumān first takes the sudden mountain for an obstacle and strikes it with his chest. | 5.1.102–109 |
| Maināka identifies himself and offers a resting place and hospitality. | 5.1.110–116, 129–130 |
| Maināka recalls that mountains once had wings, Indra cut them, and Vāyu carried him to safety; this gives him a bond with Vāyu's son. | 5.1.117–128 |
| Hanumān honours the offer but says his task is urgent; he touches Maināka and continues across the sky. | 5.1.131–136 |
| Indra praises Maināka for aiding Hanumān. | 5.1.137–143 |

**3/6-minute feasibility:** Strong. The short telling can turn on mistaken obstacle → offered welcome → gratitude without stopping. The full telling can include Ocean's reason, the mountain's wing-history and grounded flight imagery from 5.1, then close with Indra's praise. Neither edition needs Surasā or Siṃhikā, later encounters in the same sarga.

**Care and non-import:** Avoid calling Maināka a monster, a trap, or a test set by the gods: the text describes aid and hospitality. Hanumān neither rests nor rejects the kindness; his touch acknowledges it while he proceeds. Do not turn the episode into a lesson that breaks are bad; the point is his urgent task in this scene. The old wing-cutting is mythic violence that can be described without graphic detail. Existing canon already has `hanuman-reminded`, `hanuman-ring-sita`, `hanuman-tail`, and `hanuman-own-ramayana`; keep this story on the mid-flight welcome, without retelling the leap, Sītā meeting, or later Rāmāyaṇa. Check verse numbering against the chosen edition at draft stage.
