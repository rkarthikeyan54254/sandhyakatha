# Indra and Virocana at the water mirror — source-selection note

**Selection verdict:** source-grounded, but a higher-difficulty child narration. Pilot the complete teaching sequence before committing to 3/6-minute editions. The primary passage is *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* 8.7–12: [Sanskrit with section-level translation](https://adiveda.in/reading/upanishad/chandogya-upanishad-adhyaya-8/), checked against [Max Müller's public-domain English translation of the eighth prapāṭhaka](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Sacred_Books_of_the_East/Volume_1/Kh%C3%A2ndogya-upanishad/Eighth_Prap%C3%A2thaka). The latter's commentary and footnotes are interpretive notes, not narrated actions.

| Claim allowed in narration | Exact locus |
| --- | --- |
| Prajāpati says the self beyond age/death/sorrow should be sought; gods and asuras hear, and Indra and Virocana approach him with fuel in hand. | 8.7.1–2 |
| They live with him as students for 32 years; he points to the person seen in the eye, and they ask about the one seen in water or a mirror. | 8.7.3–4 |
| He has them look into water, then adorn themselves and look again; both report the reflected hair, nails, clothing and ornaments and leave satisfied. | 8.8.1–3 |
| Prajāpati says both have left without understanding. Virocana teaches that bodily self is what should be served. | 8.8.4–5 |
| Indra turns back before reaching the gods: a reflection changes as the body is blind, lame, or dies. Prajāpati asks for another 32 years of study. | 8.9.1–3 |
| Prajāpati proposes the dream self; Indra objects that dream experience can bring fear and pain, and studies another 32 years. | 8.10.1–4 |
| Prajāpati proposes dreamless sleep; Indra objects that it seems to erase awareness, then studies five more years, totaling 101 years. | 8.11.1–3 |
| Prajāpati's final account distinguishes the mortal body from the bodiless self and describes its emergence in its own form. | 8.12.1–5 |

**Non-import boundary:** Do not tell this as “the mirror reveals the true self.” The text explicitly says *both* initially misunderstood, and Indra's continuing questions supply the arc. Do not end a mirror-only version by claiming Indra has found the answer. Avoid importing a contemporary psychology lesson about self-esteem, a promise that the body is unimportant, or a later Vedānta commentary as though Prajāpati spoke it here. The source contrasts Virocana's and Indra's responses, but it does not require cartoonish or contemptuous descriptions of asuras. A short telling should retain the four stages and Indra's returns, even if philosophical explanation moves to a parent-only note.

**Length and child-facing care:** A full 650–680-word rendition is plausible from the successive reflection, dream, deep-sleep and final-teaching scenes. A 300–360-word version is possible only with very tight causal transitions and a first-hearing read-aloud trial; it must still reach 8.12 rather than imply that the reflection episode is the resolution. Start with **10+ editorial review** because identity, mortality and deep sleep are conceptually demanding, then adjust from child testing. In 8.9 the blindness/lame-body examples describe the reflection's dependence on bodily condition; they must not imply that a disabled person's worth or inner self is diminished. The ancient gods/asuras contrast and the 101 years of study belong to the text, while metaphysical variants and interpretive debates belong in a parent note.

**Canon overlap:** The current shelf already uses the *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* for `satyakama-truth` (4.4), `banyan-seed` (6.1/6.12), and `svetaketu-salt` (6.13). There is no 8.7–12 or Indra–Virocana entry, so overlap is thematic rather than a duplicate episode.
