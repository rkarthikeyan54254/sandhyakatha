# Nṛga in the Dry Well — English editorial draft

Status: **draft for source and child read-aloud review**. Neither length has been timed aloud. Witness: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.64.1–44; the events narrated below are in 1–30, with Kṛṣṇa's particular warning to his listeners in 31–44. See `../source-notes/nrga-well.md`. Proposed minimum age: 9. Parent care note: the story includes death, a long punishment as a lizard, and an unresolved dispute over a cow given by mistake. The source's closing warning concerns brāhmaṇa property in particular; the spoken ending must not turn its caste-specific framing into a falsely universal maxim.

## Short — target 3 minutes

The Yādava boys had played until they were thirsty. They went looking for water, but the well they found was dry. At the bottom was something stranger than water: a lizard so large that it looked almost like a little hill.

They felt sorry for it. They lowered straps, then ropes, and pulled. Nothing moved. So they ran to Kṛṣṇa. He came to the well, reached down with his left hand, and lifted the creature out. At his touch, the lizard became a shining man.

Kṛṣṇa asked who he was. The man bowed. _I was King Nṛga,_ he said. _I gave away more cows than I could count. But one gift went wrong._

A cow belonging to a brāhmaṇa had wandered into the king's herd. Nṛga did not know she was there. He gave her to another brāhmaṇa. The first man saw his cow being led away and called out that she was his. The second said the king had given her to him. Both came to Nṛga.

The king offered a hundred thousand fine cows in exchange for that one. He said he had made the gift without knowing the truth. Neither man accepted. Each went away, and the matter stayed unsettled.

After Nṛga died, Yama asked whether he wished to meet the results of his good deeds or this wrong first. Nṛga chose the difficult result first. He became the lizard in the well. Through all that time, he remembered who he had been.

Now the boys watched him standing before Kṛṣṇa. Nṛga bowed again. With Kṛṣṇa's permission, he went away in a heavenly vehicle.

Kṛṣṇa then spoke seriously to those with him about taking property that belonged to a brāhmaṇa, even without knowing it. The cow had not been Nṛga's to give. His great gifts had not made that one cow disappear from the story.


## Full — target 6 minutes

Sāmba, Pradyumna, and other Yādava boys played in a small forest until thirst sent them looking for water.

They found a well. It was dry. Far below sat a lizard so enormous it seemed almost as big as a hill. It could not climb out.

The boys wanted to help. First they tried leather straps. Then they tried woven ropes. They pulled and pulled, but the creature did not rise. At last they went to Kṛṣṇa and told him what they had found.

Kṛṣṇa came with them to the dry well. He looked down, reached in with his left hand, and lifted the great lizard out. The moment his hand touched it, the creature's form changed. A man stood there, clothed and ornamented like a being from heaven. The boys who had struggled with the ropes could now see him standing beside the well.

Kṛṣṇa knew more than he said, but he asked the stranger to tell everyone who he was and how he had come to be there. The man bowed. He was Nṛga, once a king, a son of Ikṣvāku.

Nṛga had given many gifts. He remembered cows with calves, cows whose horns had been gilded and whose hooves were covered in silver. He had given them to brāhmaṇas in need. How many cows? He spoke of numbers as great as the grains of sand or drops of rain.

But among all those gifts there had been one cow that was not his.

She belonged to another brāhmaṇa. She had strayed into the king's herd without Nṛga knowing. When he gave cows away, he gave her to a second man. As that man led her home, her owner saw her. _That is my cow,_ he said. _The king gave her to me,_ replied the man who had received her.

The two men came before Nṛga. Each had a claim to the same animal. One had lost his cow. The other had accepted a gift from the king. Nṛga heard them and was bewildered. What could he do that would put this right for both of them?

He offered a hundred thousand of his best cows in exchange for that single cow. He asked both men to show him mercy. He had given her away without knowing she was another person's, he told them. But the man who had received her would not exchange her. The original owner refused the offered cows too. Both left. Neither man gave way. Nṛga had offered much, but the cow was still claimed by both.

Later Nṛga died. Yama's messengers brought him before Yama, who spoke of the many good results waiting for him from his gifts. Then Yama gave him a choice: would he experience those good results first, or the painful result of the mistaken gift? Nṛga chose the painful one first.

He fell into the body of a lizard. Yet he did not forget his earlier life. He remembered who he had been through that strange life.

And then came a day when some thirsty boys looked down into a dry well. Their ropes could not lift him, but Kṛṣṇa's hand did. Standing once more in a human-shaped, shining form, Nṛga praised Kṛṣṇa. He asked to depart. Kṛṣṇa gave his permission, and Nṛga bowed and entered a heavenly vehicle while the others watched.

Kṛṣṇa spoke next to his companions. His warning was about the property of a brāhmaṇa, even when someone takes it without knowing. Nṛga had been generous beyond counting, but he had also given away a cow that belonged to someone else. Nṛga had offered more cows than most people could count, yet he had watched both men walk away. The one disputed cow remained in his account beside all those gifts.

The well was empty of water. When the boys looked into it, they found a question that had followed Nṛga there for a very long time.

## Parent-facing close and questions (draft)

- Open question: If you had been standing beside Nṛga and the two men, what would you have wanted each person to hear?
- Seed, only if needed: A generous act cannot give away what was not ours to give.
- If they ask, “Why did he become a lizard for a mistake?”: The *Bhāgavata* says the cow entered his herd without his knowledge, yet the dispute remained unresolved. Yama offered Nṛga a choice of which result to face first, and he chose the painful one. The text does not ask us to call the punishment proportionate; we can notice how severe it is.
- If they ask, “Why didn't they accept all those other cows?”: The passage tells us both refused, but does not give either man's private reasoning. One had lost his own cow; the other had received that very cow as a gift. We should not invent a settlement the source does not show.
- If they ask, “Was Kṛṣṇa warning everyone about any property?”: His speech here specifically warns his royal listeners about taking a brāhmaṇa's property, even unknowingly. We can discuss care with others' belongings without erasing that particular setting of the source.
