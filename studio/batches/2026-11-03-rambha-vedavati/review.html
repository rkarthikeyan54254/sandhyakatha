Rāvaṇa’s Assault on Rambhā and the Sevenfold Curse

en · rambha-sevenfold-curse · IN REVIEW

She had named the man she loved, asked to be released, and refused. Rāvaṇa ignored her. The curse came afterward.

Rāvaṇa rapes Rambhā after she asks to leave; no graphic description. She seeks pardon although she did nothing wrong. A curse threatens to split his head in seven. Read privately first; this is for older readers with parental opt-in.

Follows Uttara 26. Nalakūbara’s seven-piece curse after the assault on Rambhā is separate from Brahmā’s hundred-piece curse after the assault on Puñjikasthalā in Yuddha 13. Verse numbers vary; no complete recension collation is claimed.

For the parent: why we condemn Rāvaṇa’s conduct

Rāvaṇa commits evil here: he hears Rambhā ask to leave and rapes her despite her refusal. This is a deliberate abuse of power. His power cannot excuse it. That is our moral judgement of the conduct the passage describes, not an invented quotation from the text.

Uttara Kāṇḍa 26.28–55; claim ledger 2–5
Short · target 3 minutes · actual timing pending

On a moonlit night, the army of Rāvaṇa rested on Kailāsa. Among the flowers and music of the mountain, an apsarā named Rambhā passed the sleeping soldiers.

She was going to meet Nalakūbara, whom she loved. Rāvaṇa saw her, stopped her, and asked her to stay with him instead.

Rambhā refused. She told him that Nalakūbara was the son of his brother Kubera; by their family relationship she was Rāvaṇa's daughter-in-law.

She asked him to protect her, as an elder should, and let her go. The man she had chosen was waiting for her.

◇

Rāvaṇa argued that apsarās need not be bound to one partner. He paid no attention to the plain fact that Rambhā did not want him.

He overpowered and raped her. Her refusal had been clear, and nothing about his position gave him permission.

Rambhā had tried more than one way to be heard. She had asked for protection, explained their family connection, and told him who was waiting for her. He refused to respect any of it.

Shaken, Rambhā went to Nalakūbara. She told him exactly what had happened. She even asked his pardon, although the wrong was not hers.

◇

Nalakūbara listened, entered meditation, and understood the truth. He took water in his hands and pronounced a curse upon Rāvaṇa.

If you ever again force yourself upon a woman who does not want you, your head will split into seven pieces.

The curse was not against Rambhā. It was against the one who had harmed her.

The gods rejoiced. When Rāvaṇa learned what had been said, he was afraid, and the text says he no longer forced sexual union on unwilling women.

Other women he had carried away rejoiced when they heard this. The curse gave them a protection that his power had denied them.

Rambhā had said no before the assault. She should not have needed a curse to make that answer matter.

The army of Rāvaṇa had halted on Kailāsa. The sun had gone down, and the soldiers slept while the moon lit the mountain forests.

There were flowering trees, pools of lotuses, and the waters of the Mandākinī. Music carried from the singers and dancers who lived among the mountains.

Rāvaṇa sat above the camp. Then he saw an apsarā passing through the sleeping army, wearing fresh flowers and ornaments for a meeting she had chosen.

She wore a dark blue cloth and flowers in her hair. A jewelled belt and garlands adorned her. She had dressed to meet the man she loved, and she was passing through the ranks of the army on her way to him.

Her name was Rambhā. She was on her way to Nalakūbara, whom she loved and who was waiting for her.

◇

Rāvaṇa rose and stopped her. He asked where she was going, praised her beauty, and urged her to remain with him instead.

Rambhā was frightened. She folded her hands and appealed to the duty he owed her: You should protect me from violence, not threaten me with it.

She told him he was an elder in her family. Nalakūbara was the son of Kubera, and Kubera was Rāvaṇa's brother. By that relationship, Rambhā was his daughter-in-law.

Rāvaṇa questioned her claim. She explained it again, naming the man she had come to meet and saying that her heart was set on him alone.

Nalakūbara was waiting. She had dressed for him; she had given her word to him. She asked Rāvaṇa to let her go and to follow the conduct expected of a guardian of his family.

◇

Rāvaṇa would not accept her refusal. He argued that apsarās did not follow the same rules of marriage as women who had only one husband.

But Rambhā had not come to him. She had asked to leave. She had explained whose company she wanted, and it was not his.

He ignored her pleas and raped her. She had asked him to let her go; he had heard her and refused.

When she could leave, Rambhā went to Nalakūbara. Her flowers and ornaments were disturbed. She was shaking.

Nalakūbara asked why she had come to him in such distress. Rambhā told him how Rāvaṇa had stopped her, questioned her, and refused to listen even after she spoke of their family relationship.

She told him that Rāvaṇa had overpowered her. She even asked Nalakūbara to pardon her, though the wrong had been done to her, not by her.

She had first appealed to his duty as an elder. Then she had explained their kinship. Then she had spoken of the man waiting for her.

Each appeal had been clear; none had stopped Rāvaṇa.

◇

Nalakūbara heard her account. He entered meditation and established what had happened. He became angry at the violence committed against her.

Taking water in his hands, he spoke a curse against Rāvaṇa: If you ever again force yourself on a woman who does not want you, your head will break into seven pieces.

It was not a punishment placed upon Rambhā. The curse fell upon the man who had refused to respect her will.

The tale says that divine drums sounded and flowers fell from the sky when the curse was pronounced. Those who understood what lay ahead knew that Rāvaṇa had been checked.

When the warning reached Rāvaṇa, he was shaken. The text says he thereafter ceased to force sexual union upon women who did not desire him.

Women whom he had already carried away against their wishes heard of the curse. They rejoiced at the protection it promised them.

◇

For the parent · not aloud
The separate account in Yuddha 13 names Brahmā and Puñjikasthalā. Do not present Nalakūbara’s curse as the sole explanation for Sītā’s protection.

For this night on Kailāsa, the story remains Rambhā's: she refused him, she told the truth of what followed, and the consequence was placed where it belonged.

The mountain had heard Rāvaṇa speak as though power gave him a right. Rambhā had said no. It was his deed, not her refusal, that brought the curse.

After the telling

Have you ever told someone to stop, or heard someone else say stop? What do you think listening should look like in that moment?

A refusal is enough; the person who ignores it is responsible for the harm.

Why did Rambhā ask Nalakūbara to pardon her?

The text says she was frightened and sought his pardon after telling him what had happened. She had not chosen the assault. Asking pardon here is part of her distress, not proof that she was responsible. Nalakūbara directs the curse at Rāvaṇa, not at her.

What does it mean that Rāvaṇa raped her?

It means he forced sexual contact on Rambhā when she did not want it. She had asked him to let her go. No relationship, rank, or argument about customs can turn that refusal into permission. The text names this violence without needing a graphic description.

Is this the curse that protected Sītā?

Uttara Kāṇḍa 26 tells of Nalakūbara’s seven-piece curse after the assault on Rambhā. Yuddha Kāṇḍa 13 separately has Rāvaṇa describe Brahmā’s hundred-piece curse after he assaulted Puñjikasthalā, and he says that curse kept him from forcing Sītā. We should keep the two accounts distinct.

रम्भा ने हाथ जोड़कर रास्ता छोड़ने को कहा। रावण ने उनकी एक न सुनी।

रावण रम्भा के मना करने पर भी उनके साथ बलात्कार करता है। कोई अश्लील या वीभत्स विवरण नहीं है। रम्भा क्षमा माँगती हैं, जबकि उनका कोई दोष नहीं था। शाप में सिर के सात टुकड़े होने की बात है। पहले स्वयं पढ़ें; बड़े बच्चों के लिए, माता-पिता की सहमति से ही।

उत्तरकाण्ड के सर्ग २६ की कथा। यहाँ नलकूबर का शाप है। युद्धकाण्ड के सर्ग १३ में पुंजिकस्थला पर अत्याचार के बाद ब्रह्मा का अलग शाप आता है। दोनों को मिलाया नहीं गया है। संस्करणों में श्लोक-संख्या बदलती है।

माता-पिता के लिए: रावण के आचरण की निंदा क्यों

यहाँ रावण का आचरण दुष्टतापूर्ण है। रम्भा उसे मना करती हैं और जाने देने को कहती हैं। फिर भी वह उनके साथ बलात्कार करता है। उसकी शक्ति इस अत्याचार का बहाना नहीं हो सकती। यह वर्णित कर्मों पर हमारा नैतिक निर्णय है, ग्रंथ से लिया हुआ उद्धरण नहीं।

कैलास पर्वत पर रात उतर आई थी। रावण की सेना सो रही थी। चाँदनी में फूल खिले थे और दूर से गाने की आवाज़ आ रही थी।

उसी रास्ते से अप्सरा रम्भा जा रही थीं। वे नलकूबर से मिलने निकली थीं और उन्हीं के लिए सजकर आई थीं। रावण ने उन्हें देखा और रोक लिया।

रम्भा ने हाथ जोड़ दिए। उन्होंने कहा कि उसे उनकी रक्षा करनी चाहिए, उन्हें डराना नहीं चाहिए। वे नलकूबर के पास जाना चाहती थीं।

नलकूबर कुबेर के पुत्र थे और कुबेर रावण के भाई थे। इस नाते रम्भा ने अपने को रावण की पुत्रवधू बताया। उन्होंने उससे रास्ता छोड़ने को कहा।

रावण ने उनका संबंध मानने से इनकार किया, तो उन्होंने फिर समझाया। नलकूबर उनकी प्रतीक्षा कर रहे थे। वे उन्हीं से मिलने जा रही थीं, किसी और से नहीं।

रावण कहने लगा कि अप्सराओं का कोई एक पति नहीं होता। उसने इसी बात का सहारा लेकर रम्भा की विनती ठुकरा दी।

रम्भा ने उसे मना किया था। फिर भी रावण ने उनके साथ बलात्कार किया।

छूटने पर रम्भा काँपती हुई नलकूबर के पास पहुँचीं। उनके फूल और आभूषण अस्त-व्यस्त थे। उन्होंने सारी बात बताई—कैसे रोका गया, कैसे उन्होंने रिश्ता समझाया और कैसे रावण ने उनकी बात नहीं मानी।

उन्होंने नलकूबर से क्षमा भी माँगी। जबकि उनके साथ ज़बरदस्ती हुई थी; दोष उनका नहीं था।

नलकूबर ने उनकी बात सुनी। ध्यान में उन्होंने घटना की सच्चाई जानी। फिर जल लेकर रावण को शाप दिया।

यदि फिर कभी तुम किसी स्त्री की इच्छा के विरुद्ध उसके साथ ज़बरदस्ती करोगे, तो तुम्हारे सिर के सात टुकड़े हो जाएँगे।

देवताओं की दुंदुभियाँ बज उठीं और आकाश से फूल बरसे। शाप सुनकर रावण डर गया। इसके बाद उसने अनिच्छुक स्त्रियों के साथ ज़बरदस्ती यौन संबंध बनाना छोड़ दिया।

जिन स्त्रियों को वह पहले हरकर लाया था, उन्हें भी यह समाचार मिला। उन्होंने राहत पाई।

रम्भा को दंड नहीं मिला। शाप उस पर पड़ा जिसने उनकी बात सुनकर भी ज़बरदस्ती की थी।

सूरज डूब चुका था। कैलास पर्वत पर रावण ने अपनी सेना को रात भर ठहरने दिया। हथियारों से सुसज्जित सैनिक सो गए और पहाड़ पर चाँदनी फैल गई।

वन में फूल खिले थे। कमल से भरे सरोवर और मंदाकिनी का जल चाँदनी में दिखाई दे रहे थे। हवा में फूलों की महक थी।

किन्नरों और अप्सराओं के गीत सुनाई दे रहे थे। रावण पर्वत पर बैठा आसपास देख रहा था। तभी उसकी नज़र सेना के बीच से जाती एक अप्सरा पर पड़ी।

वे रम्भा थीं। बालों में फूल थे, गले में मालाएँ और शरीर पर आभूषण। वे नलकूबर से मिलने जा रही थीं, जिन्हें वे चाहती थीं।

रावण उठा और उसने उनका रास्ता रोक लिया। उसने पूछा कि वे किससे मिलने जा रही हैं। फिर अपने साथ ठहरने को कहा।

रम्भा डर गईं। हाथ जोड़कर उन्होंने रावण से कहा कि बड़े होने के नाते उसका काम उनकी रक्षा करना है। उसे स्वयं उनके लिए संकट नहीं बनना चाहिए।

उन्होंने अपने संबंध की बात समझाई। नलकूबर कुबेर के पुत्र थे। कुबेर रावण के भाई थे। इसलिए रम्भा ने कहा कि धर्म और संबंध के नाते वे उसकी पुत्रवधू हैं।

रावण ने उनकी बात पर सवाल उठाया। रम्भा ने फिर नलकूबर का नाम लिया। वे उसी से मिलने निकली थीं; उसी के लिए सजी थीं।

नलकूबर उनकी राह देख रहे थे। रम्भा ने रावण से उन्हें जाने देने को कहा। उन्होंने उससे अपने परिवार के बड़े जैसा आचरण करने की विनती की।

रावण ने उत्तर दिया कि अप्सराएँ एक पति से बँधी नहीं होतीं। उसने इस तर्क से उनका संबंध और उनकी इच्छा, दोनों को अनदेखा कर दिया।

रम्भा उसके पास नहीं आई थीं। उन्होंने उसके साथ रुकने की इच्छा नहीं जताई थी। वे बार-बार जाने देने को कह रही थीं।

लेकिन रावण ने उनकी विनती नहीं मानी। उसने उनके साथ बलात्कार किया।

जब वे उससे छूटीं, तो नलकूबर के पास पहुँचीं। उनकी मालाएँ और आभूषण बिगड़ गए थे। वे काँप रही थीं।

नलकूबर ने उनका हाल देखकर पूछा कि क्या हुआ। रम्भा ने शुरू से सब बताया। रावण ने उन्हें कैसे रोका, क्या कहा और उनके समझाने पर भी कैसे नहीं माना।

उन्होंने बताया कि वे अपनी इच्छा से उसके पास नहीं रुकी थीं। उसने उनके साथ ज़बरदस्ती की थी। बोलते-बोलते उन्होंने नलकूबर से क्षमा भी माँगी।

उनका कोई दोष नहीं था। उन्होंने रावण से सुरक्षा माँगी थी, संबंध समझाया था और जाने देने को कहा था। उसने इनमें से कोई बात नहीं मानी।

அவளுடைய வேண்டுகோளை ராவணன் கேட்கவில்லை. அவளைப் பாலியல் வன்கொடுமைக்கு ஆளாக்கினான்.

அவனிடமிருந்து விடுபட்டபின் ரம்பை நளகூபரனிடம் சென்றாள். மலர்களும் அணிகலன்களும் கலைந்திருந்தன. அவள் உடல் நடுங்கியது.

அவள் வந்த கோலத்தைப் பார்த்த நளகூபரன் என்ன நடந்தது என்று கேட்டான். ரம்பை நடந்ததையெல்லாம் சொன்னாள்.

ராவணன் தன்னை நிறுத்தியதைச் சொன்னாள். அவனிடம் பாதுகாப்பு கேட்டதையும் உறவுமுறையை விளக்கியதையும் சொன்னாள். அவன் தன்னை வற்புறுத்தியதையும் மறைக்கவில்லை.

அவள் நளகூபரனிடம் மன்னிப்பும் கேட்டாள். ஆனால் அவள் தவறு செய்திருக்கவில்லை. தன்னை விட்டுவிடும்படி சொன்ன அவளுக்குத்தான் கொடுமை நடந்திருந்தது.

நளகூபரன் அவள் சொல்வதைக் கேட்டான். தியானத்தில் ஆழ்ந்து நடந்ததை அறிந்தான். ரம்பைக்குச் செய்யப்பட்ட கொடுமையால் அவன் சினமடைந்தான்.

நீரை ஏந்தி ராவணனைச் சபித்தான்: இனி நீ விரும்பாத ஒரு பெண்ணை வற்புறுத்தினால், உன் தலை ஏழு துண்டுகளாகப் பிளக்கும்.

சாபம் ரம்பையைத் தண்டிக்கவில்லை. அவள் விருப்பத்தை மீறித் துன்புறுத்திய ராவணனைக் கட்டுப்படுத்தியது.

அப்போது தேவ துந்துபிகள் முழங்கின. வானிலிருந்து மலர்கள் பொழிந்தன. தேவர்கள் மகிழ்ந்தனர்.

இந்தச் செய்தி ராவணனை அடைந்ததும் அவன் அஞ்சினான். அதன்பின் தன்னை விரும்பாத பெண்களுடன் வலுக்கட்டாயமாகப் பாலுறவு கொள்வதை அவன் விட்டதாகக் கதை கூறுகிறது.

ராவணன் ஏற்கெனவே பல பெண்களைக் கடத்தி வந்திருந்தான். அவர்களும் சாபத்தைப் பற்றிக் கேள்விப்பட்டனர். அது தங்களுக்குப் பாதுகாப்பாக இருக்கும் என்று மகிழ்ந்தனர்.

ரம்பை அச்சத்தோடும் துயரத்தோடும் நளகூபரனை அடைந்திருந்தாள். தனக்கு நடந்த கொடுமையை அவனிடம் சொல்லியிருந்தாள்.

அவள் தன்னை விட்டுவிடும்படி கேட்டதை ராவணன் மதிக்கவில்லை. அவன் செய்த கொடுமைக்கே சாபம் வந்தது; அவள் மறுத்ததற்காக அல்ல.

பேசிப் பார்க்கலாம்

நீங்கள் வேண்டாம் என்று சொல்லும்போது, மற்றவர் எப்படி நடந்துகொண்டால் உங்கள் சொல்லை மதிப்பதாக உணர்வீர்கள்?

வேண்டாம் என்றால் நிறுத்த வேண்டும். மீறித் துன்புறுத்தியவரிடமே தவறு இருக்கிறது.

தவறு செய்யாத ரம்பை ஏன் மன்னிப்புக் கேட்டாள்?

நடந்ததைச் சொல்லும்போது ரம்பை அச்சத்தில் இருந்ததாகவும் மன்னிப்புக் கேட்டதாகவும் நூல் கூறுகிறது. அதனால் அவள்மீது தவறு இருந்ததாக ஆகாது. அவள் சம்மதிக்கவில்லை. நளகூபரன் சபித்ததும் ராவணனைத்தான்; ரம்பையை அல்ல.

பாலியல் வன்கொடுமை என்றால் என்ன?

ஒருவர் விரும்பாதபோது அவரைக் கட்டாயப்படுத்திப் பாலுறவு கொள்வது பாலியல் வன்கொடுமை. ரம்பை தன்னை விட்டுவிடும்படி சொல்லியிருந்தாள். பதவியோ வலிமையோ உறவுமுறையோ அவளுடைய மறுப்பை மீறும் உரிமையை ராவணனுக்குத் தராது.

சீதையைக் காத்த சாபம் இதுதானா?

இந்த அத்தியாயத்தில் ரம்பையைத் துன்புறுத்திய ராவணனை நளகூபரன் சபிக்கிறான்; தலை ஏழாகப் பிளக்கும் என்கிறான். யுத்த காண்டம் பதின்மூன்றில் ராவணன் வேறொரு சாபத்தைச் சொல்கிறான். புஞ்சிகஸ்தலையைத் துன்புறுத்தியதற்காகப் பிரம்மா கொடுத்த அந்தச் சாபத்தில் தலை நூறாகப் பிளக்கும் என வருகிறது. இரு சம்பவங்களையும் ஒன்றாகச் சொல்லக்கூடாது.

Rāvaṇa’s Assault on Vedavatī and Her Vow

en · vedavati-hair-and-vow · IN REVIEW

In a forest, she refused Rāvaṇa and defended her vow. When he seized her hair, she made a promise the Rāmāyaṇa would remember.

Rāvaṇa seizes Vedavatī’s hair. She cuts it free, then enters fire and dies. Her father is murdered; her mother also enters fire. No graphic detail. Read privately first. Assault does not make a victim impure; this ending is not conduct to imitate.

Follows Shastri’s Uttara 17 ending, identifying Vedavatī with Sītā. The inspected Sanskrit web text adds a lotus-and-sea episode before Janaka finds her. That episode is not narrated here. Yuddha 60.10 also identifies her with Sītā; numbering varies.

For the parent: why we condemn Rāvaṇa’s conduct

Rāvaṇa commits evil here: after Vedavatī refuses him and asks him to leave, he disregards her wishes and seizes her hair. His power gives him no right to assault her. This passage describes assault; it does not describe completed rape. That distinction strengthens an honest condemnation.

Uttara Kāṇḍa 17.16–29; claim ledger 3–5
Short · target 3 minutes · actual timing pending

In the Himālayas, a young ascetic named Vedavatī practised tapas. She had devoted herself to Nārāyaṇa, or Viṣṇu, and would accept no other husband.

Her father, the sage Kuśadhvaja, had wished the same. Many had asked to marry her, but he had refused them. A daitya named Śambhu killed him after that refusal; her mother entered the funeral fire.

Still Vedavatī continued her vow. One day, as she lived in the forest, Rāvaṇa arrived and asked why she had chosen such a hard life.

She welcomed him as a visitor, told him who she was, explained her devotion to Viṣṇu, and asked him to go.

Rāvaṇa would not listen. He offered her life beside him as ruler of Laṅkā and spoke proudly against Viṣṇu.

Vedavatī rejected his words. She had made her choice, and neither his power nor his offers changed it.

◇

Then Rāvaṇa seized her hair. Angry at being assaulted, she cut off the hair he held. Her hand became sharp as a sword.

What happened next is painful: Vedavatī prepared a fire and chose to enter it. Before she did, she told Rāvaṇa that she would return in another birth to bring about his destruction.

She would not use up her tapas by cursing him at once. Instead, she asked that her gifts, sacrifices and austerities lead to a worthy new birth.

Then she entered the flames. Flowers fell from above.

◇

The Rāmāyaṇa tells us the next part. Vedavatī was born again as Sītā, whom Janaka found in a ploughed field.

Years later, in Laṅkā, Rāvaṇa himself remembered the wrong he had done Vedavatī and recognised that Sītā had come into the world. He spoke of her after returning from battle against Rāma.

Rāvaṇa had tried to break Vedavatī's decision. Her vow remained part of the story long after he left that forest.

Full · target 6 minutes · actual timing pending

In a forest near the Himālayas, Vedavatī lived as an ascetic. She wore a black antelope skin and kept her hair in matted locks.

She had not come there by accident. Her father, the sage Kuśadhvaja, had devoted himself to the Vedas and wished that his daughter should be united with Viṣṇu.

Many powerful beings had once sought her hand. Her father refused those proposals because he had made his choice for Viṣṇu.

A proud daitya named Śambhu was enraged by the refusal. He killed Kuśadhvaja while the sage slept. Vedavatī's mother then entered the funeral fire holding her husband.

Vedavatī continued her own vow. Her heart, she said, was given to Nārāyaṇa, another name for Viṣṇu, and she undertook severe tapas for that purpose.

◇

One day Rāvaṇa entered the forest. He saw the young ascetic and wondered why someone of her age and beauty was living with such austerity.

He asked whose daughter she was and why she had chosen this life. Vedavatī received him with the hospitality due to a visitor before explaining her family and her vow.

She had received her visitor with the hospitality due to a guest. When she spoke of her father, she named him as a sage devoted to study. His wish for her marriage was the wish she herself continued to hold.

She told him about her father, the marriage proposals, the murder, and the penance she now performed. She also told him she knew who he was.

Then she asked Rāvaṇa to leave. Her purpose was not hidden and her decision was not open for him to make.

But Rāvaṇa stepped down from his chariot. He called himself the ruler of Laṅkā, offered wealth and pleasure, and told her to become his consort.

He also spoke contemptuously of Viṣṇu, claiming to be greater in power. He treated her chosen devotion as something he was entitled to overturn.

Vedavatī would not accept those words. She defended Viṣṇu and rebuked Rāvaṇa for speaking against the Lord honoured by all the worlds.

◇

Then Rāvaṇa seized her by the hair. She had already told him whom she wished to marry and asked him to leave. Now he held her against her will.

Vedavatī was furious. With her own hand, which took on the sharpness of a sword, she cut off the hair he had seized.

She did not turn to him for permission to be free. She cut away what he held and spoke of the injury he had done.

What followed is difficult. Vedavatī said that after his violation she would not continue living in that body. She prepared a fire and resolved to enter it before his eyes.

She told Rāvaṇa that because he had insulted and assaulted her in the forest, she would be born again for his destruction.

She would not spend the power of her tapas on a curse there and then. Instead she prayed that the good she had done through gifts, sacrifice, and austerity would allow her a new and worthy birth.

◇

Vedavatī entered the fire she had kindled. The account says that flowers fell from above.

For the parent · not aloud
Her death is not advice for anyone who has been harmed. Being assaulted does not make a person impure. Keep that distinction clear when discussing this sacred narrative.

The sage who recounts the episode then tells Rāma who Vedavatī became: she was born again as Sītā, the daughter of Janaka.

Janaka found Sītā as he ploughed a field. In the narrative, this birth was the answer to Vedavatī's declared intention to return and become the cause of Rāvaṇa's fall.

Later, when Rāvaṇa suffered defeat at the hands of Rāma, he himself remembered Vedavatī. In the Yuddha Kāṇḍa, he spoke of the wrong he had done her and recognised Sītā as her return.

◇

The tale names Vedavatī as the woman who returned, not as someone forgotten in the forest. Its next turn belongs to Sītā, who is later taken to Laṅkā by the same Rāvaṇa.

Rāvaṇa thought he had interrupted a vow in a lonely forest. The Rāmāyaṇa carries that vow forward into the life of Sītā and the fall of Rāvaṇa.

After the telling

Have you ever held on to a promise after someone tried to change your mind? What helped you decide what was yours to choose?

No amount of power gave Rāvaṇa the right to make Vedavatī’s choice for her.

Did Rāvaṇa rape Vedavatī?

In Uttara Kāṇḍa 17 he tries to persuade her to become his consort and, after her refusal, seizes her hair. The passage does not describe completed rape. Grabbing her hair against her will is itself a violent assault.

Why did she enter the fire? Should someone harmed do the same?

The Rāmāyaṇa says she chose to enter the fire after Rāvaṇa seized her hair, and that she vowed to be reborn for his destruction. This is a grave and troubling part of this specific sacred narrative, not advice for anyone who has been harmed. A person who has been assaulted is not impure and does not owe anyone death or punishment. In our lives, they deserve safety, support and care.

Was Vedavatī really Sītā?

The Uttara Kāṇḍa explicitly identifies Vedavatī’s next birth as Sītā, found by Janaka in a ploughed field. Rāvaṇa also recalls that connection in Yuddha Kāṇḍa 6.60.10. The telling follows those passages without adding details from other retellings.

वेदवती पर रावण का अत्याचार

रावण ने वेदवती के बाल पकड़ लिए। उन्होंने वे बाल काट दिए और उसके विनाश के लिए फिर जन्म लेने का संकल्प किया।

रावण वेदवती के बाल पकड़ता है। वे बाल काटकर अलग होती हैं, फिर अग्नि में प्रवेश करके प्राण त्याग देती हैं। पिता की हत्या और माता का अग्नि-प्रवेश भी आता है। पहले स्वयं पढ़ें। अत्याचार सहने वाला अपवित्र नहीं होता; यह प्रसंग अनुकरण की सीख नहीं है।

यह कथा शास्त्री के उत्तरकाण्ड सर्ग १७ के पाठानुसार है। जाँचे गए संस्कृत वेब-पाठ में सीता के जन्म से पहले कमल और समुद्र का अतिरिक्त प्रसंग है; यहाँ वह नहीं जोड़ा गया है। युद्धकाण्ड ६०.१० में भी वेदवती और सीता का संबंध आता है।

माता-पिता के लिए: रावण के आचरण की निंदा क्यों

वेदवती के मना करने और चले जाने को कहने पर भी रावण उनके बाल पकड़ता है। यह दुष्टता और ज़बरदस्ती है। उसकी शक्ति उसे ऐसा अधिकार नहीं देती। इस प्रसंग में हिंसक अत्याचार है; बलात्कार का वर्णन नहीं है। उसकी निंदा करते समय भी यह अंतर सही रखना चाहिए।

நாம் தேர்ந்தெடுத்த உத்தர காண்டப் பாடம் வேதவதியை ஜனகரின் மகள் சீதையாக அடையாளம் காட்டுகிறது. யுத்த காண்டம் அறுபதாம் சர்க்கத்தின் பத்தாம் பாடலிலும் ராவணன் இந்தத் தொடர்பை நினைவுகூர்கிறான். இடையில் நடக்கும் நிகழ்வுகள் பாடங்களுக்கேற்ப மாறுகின்றன. வேறு பாடத்தின் நிகழ்வுகளை இந்தக் கதையில் கலக்கவில்லை.
