# Ṛbhu and Nidāgha — source ledger

Checked 2026-10-08. Primary witness: H. H. Wilson, *The Vishnu Purana*, 1840, Book II chapters XV–XVI; online transcription [2.15](https://scriptures.redzambala.com/vishnu-purana/vishnu-purana-book-2-chapter-15.html), [2.16](https://scriptures.redzambala.com/vishnu-purana/vishnu-purana-book-2-chapter-16.html). Sanskrit cross-check of the second encounter: [2.16](https://www.sanskritsahitya.org/vishnupuranam/2.16), Sanskrit verses only, not its AI summaries. Wilson's chapter XV heading says the pupil becomes a prince; the narrated text identifies the pupil as Pulastya's son and the prince as the outer-frame listener. Do NOT copy that heading's claim.

## Canonical claims and scene map

- R1 — II.15 opening: Ṛbhu, son of Brahmā, teaches Nidāgha, son of Pulastya. Nidāgha lives by the Devīkā in Vīranagara. The outer story is Bharata teaching a king; this is a nested example, not the Bhāgavata fawn episode.
- R2 — II.15 first visit: after a thousand divine years, the teacher comes to the pupil's doorway after the Viśvadeva rite. Nidāgha welcomes him, washes hands/feet, offers food. Guest requests sweet food; pupil's wife prepares it.
- R3 — II.15 meal dialogue: Nidāgha asks about hunger, satisfaction and the guest's home/journey. Ṛbhu distinguishes bodily hunger/thirst and mental satisfaction from the Self. Taste changes with hunger/fullness; food sustains the earth-formed body. He identifies himself and teaches the undivided nature of Vāsudeva.
- R4 — II.16 opening: after another thousand years Ṛbhu returns. Nidāgha is carrying firewood and sacred grass, thirsty, standing away from the king's crowd. Teacher approaches as a stranger; do not invent a disguise or memory-loss curse.
- R5 — II.16 dialogue: king on elephant; attendants around. Teacher asks which is king and which elephant, then what above/below mean. Nidāgha climbs onto Ṛbhu to demonstrate; retain this inconvenient action without making it a game for children.
- R6 — II.16 turn: teacher asks who is “you” and who “I.” Nidāgha recognizes his teacher by the teaching of unity, bows, and receives the confirmation.
- R7 — II.16 ending: teacher leaves; Nidāgha sees all beings as himself and reaches liberation. The outer teaching names Achyuta/Viṣṇu. Do not reduce non-duality to identical bodies or to a lesson about king and elephant exchanging jobs.

We follow that account rather than combining it with the later Ṛbhu Gītā.” The thousand-year intervals stay in the parent note in short; full includes them. No added name for the wife, no invented reaction from the king or crowd, no claim everyone understood the demonstration.

## Child/editorial boundaries

Proposed age 9+, ungated. Care: pupil climbs onto an adult to make a point; discuss rather than imitate. Hunger is real; Self-teaching never excuses denying someone food. Short has its own two-question approach; full includes both visits and the food/taste teaching. Final doctrine stays Viṣṇu-grounded. No adult scholarship inside the spoken lane. Questions invite experience, not a quiz. Self-audit is not human approval. English read-aloud, Hindi/Tamil native editing, measured timings and art review pending.

## Art brief

Use R4: two adult sages standing beside a royal procession, Nidāgha carrying firewood/grass; elephant and seated ruler in background. This is one moment. No riding the teacher in the selected hero, no student depicted as a child, no written labels, no illustration of Brahman as a new physical object. Incidental architecture/clothing are artistic reconstruction, not sourced claims.
