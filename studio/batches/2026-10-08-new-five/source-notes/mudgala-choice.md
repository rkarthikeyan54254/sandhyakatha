# Mudgala — source ledger

Checked 2026-10-08. Witness: Kisari Mohan Ganguli, *The Mahabharata* (1883–1896), Vana Parva, sections CCLVIII–CCLIX, printed pp. 510–514: [258](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m03/m03258.htm), [259](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m03/m03259.htm). This is Ganguli section numbering, not a claimed critical-edition locus. Sanskrit contextual check: [3.246.11](https://enjoylearningsanskrit.com/scriptures/mahabharata/book-3/chapter-246/verse-11/) and [3.246.30](https://enjoylearningsanskrit.com/scriptures/mahabharata/book-3/chapter-246/verse-30/), Sanskrit only; numbering differs. No critical-edition collation claimed.

## Claims and scenes

- M1 — CCLVIII opening: Vyāsa tells Yudhiṣṭhira about Mudgala, living at Kurukṣetra with wife and son. He gleans ears/grains left after harvest; collects a droṇa over a fortnight, makes new/full moon offerings, feeds guests, takes what remains. The translation's fortnight phrasing is awkward: do not assert a daily menu or “six consecutive days.”
- M2 — CCLVIII miracle: food increases when guests arrive; hundreds of learned brāhmaṇas fed; Indra and devas attend his offerings. Include food increase in both lengths; do not replace with modern meal-count arithmetic.
- M3 — CCLVIII test: Durvāsas comes insulting, asks for food, eats the entire supply, smears leftovers on his body, leaves. Returns on SIX successive feeding occasions; Mudgala goes hungry and gleans again, without anger or agitation. Wife and son present in account; do not invent their consent, approval, or hunger dialogue.
- M4 — CCLVIII ending: sage praises restraint and generosity, declares bodily ascent to heaven. Messenger arrives in decorated, fragrant car with bells and swans/cranes; invites Mudgala. Mudgala asks both heaven's happiness AND disadvantages before deciding. Seven steps establish friendship in his request; can omit without changing decision.
- M5 — CCLIX: messenger describes gardens, no hunger/thirst/fatigue, bright bodies and garlands; then exhausted merit, inability to perform new meritorious actions there, fading garlands before departure, distress/fall/return. Not eternal liberation. No equation with a cash bank account or fake tickets.
- M6 — CCLIX ending: Mudgala reflects, politely declines, asks about freedom from such defects; messenger names supreme Viṣṇu/Para Brahman, beyond attachment/pride/anger/envy. Messenger leaves. Mudgala continues gleaning, gains equanimity, meditation, knowledge and eternal liberation. Do not end with him riding to heaven or claim he stayed simply to feed his family.

## Tradition and care

Proposed `stability: variant`, for an observed material witness difference, not merely because an edition is named. Compare [Sanskrit 3.247.39–45](https://mahabharata-online.github.io/sanskrit/3/247.html) with Ganguli CCLIX, printed p. 513. The Sanskrit transcription moves from refusal to dismissal of the messenger and has `śiloñchavṛttim utsṛjya` (giving up gleaning); it does not contain the additional question/answer naming the supreme seat of Viṣṇu that is present in Ganguli. Ganguli describes him still leading the Unchha mode of life. The [CE/Ganguli chapter mapping](https://mahabharata.shreevatsa.net/) identifies 3.247 with Ganguli 259. The digital tradition's [BORI checking statement](https://bombay.indology.info/mahabharata/statement.html) is available; this is a comparison of printed/digital witnesses, not a fresh manuscript apparatus collation.

Tradition note: “We follow Ganguli's Mahābhārata, Vana sections 258–259. In that witness the messenger speaks of the supreme abode of Viṣṇu and Mudgala continues gleaning before reaching liberation. The Sanskrit 3.247 text checked here has a shorter ending and says he gives up gleaning. We keep these tellings separate.” A dictionary search returned a misleading version in which the car carries him away; rejected against the actual passage.

Age 10+, ungated proposal for parent review. Care: repeated hunger, guest consumes household food and smears leftovers. Do not praise denying children's food or say children should tolerate mistreatment. Wife/son's feelings are unstated. Source facts remain; parent note makes imitation boundary explicit. Distinguish severe adult tapas from instructions for family conduct. The source's exclusions and cosmic hierarchy are available in references; do not turn the narrator into a judge of families' beliefs. Closing question concerns examining a choice, not renouncing joy.

Art scene: M4/M5, clothed Mudgala standing beside stationary celestial car and messenger, swans/cranes, bells; no boarding, no Durvāsas in this later moment, no modern carriage wheels claimed necessary. Incidental setting/costume is reconstruction.
