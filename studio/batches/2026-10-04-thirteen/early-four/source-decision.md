## Exact full-scene follow-up: Lakṣmaṇa

A directly inspectable primary witness is now available: Gona Buddha Reddy’s Telugu *Ranganātha Rāmāyaṇamu*, Āraṇya Kāṇḍamu, in the 1942 Veturi Prabhakara Sastri / Andhra University edition ([edition index](https://www.andhrabharati.com/itihAsamulu/RanganathaRamayanamu/index.html)), also transcribed as a continuous [Wikisource canto](https://te.wikisource.org/wiki/రంగనాథరామాయణము/ఆరణ్యకాండము). Cite the canto’s running line numbers **870–1010** rather than an unverified print page. The inspectable [passage around lines 900–1000](https://te.wikisource.org/wiki/రంగనాథరామాయణము/ఆరణ్యకాండము) gives a connected scene:

1. Rāma leaves to pursue the enchanted deer, charging Lakṣmaṇa to guard Sītā (around 870).
2. Mārīca dies calling out in Lakṣmaṇa’s name; Sītā hears the cry, urges Lakṣmaṇa to go, and the two have a painful argument (about 900–930).
3. Lakṣmaṇa draws **seven lines** around the hut, tells Sītā not to cross them, invokes Agni, and departs (about 930–935).
4. Rāvaṇa arrives disguised as an ascetic (about 935–950). **The text has Sītā cross Lakṣmaṇa’s lines while honoring the apparent guest** (about 950); it does not present a separate food/alms demand as the device that makes her cross.
5. Sītā identifies herself; Rāvaṇa discloses himself, offers his kingdom, and she refuses. He then seizes her (about 950–1010).

**Draftability verdict: yes for a source-bound English 3/6-minute pair from this Telugu regional version**, subject to a Telugu-literate second check of the marked lines and a parent-facing `regional` tradition note. An [independent English rendering](https://hinduism.stackexchange.com/questions/48659/lakshmana-rekha-mythology/48698) cites *Ranganātha Rāmāyaṇa* Āraṇya Kāṇḍa 18 and agrees specifically on the seven lines, warning, and Agni invocation; the secondary source does not verify the entire later scene. The canonical title’s singular “line” and the hook’s claim that the line is absent from “the Rāmāyaṇa” require correction: this witness says seven lines, while *Vālmīki* does not narrate them. Do not import the popular one-line/alms/trapped-at-the-hut details into this version. The current canon row remains unchanged until editorial review. An [English candidate with independent 3/6-minute narrations and ledger](en/lakshmana-rekha-candidate.md) is saved; its narration word counts are 304 and 664 respectively, before close questions/seeds, giving the target labels at 110 spoken words/minute. It remains unapproved.

## Exact narrative follow-up: Mīrā

A full connected **alternative** witness exists: *Pothī Prem Ambodh*, Mīrā paracī, completed in 1693 for performance at Guru Gobind Singh’s Anandpur court, in John Stratton Hawley and Gurinder Singh Mann’s [scholarly edition/translation](https://giss.org/mann/mirabai_in_the_pothi_prem_ambodh.pdf), *Journal of Punjab Studies* 15:1–2, pp. **199–225**. The cup sequence is **chhand/dohā 26–30, journal pp. 214–216**: the king prepares sweetened liquid in a golden cup and presents it as consecrated food; Mīrā drinks, continues singing kīrtan; he offers a second cup; she is unharmed; he later approaches with a sword and sees Hari listening to her song. The source has enough events for a 3/6-minute retelling without padding.

This is **not the planned Chittor/Rāṇā account**. In the *Prem Ambodh*, Giridhar is a human husband-form of Hari, and the poisoner is Mīrā’s own father/king. Hawley and Mann explicitly call it the oldest dated substantial narrative and note its difference from Priyādās’s later *Bhaktirasabodhinī* (1712), pp. 199–200. It belongs to a Sikh courtly transmission of a Vaiṣṇava bhakta’s life. **Draftability verdict: conditional only if the editor deliberately chooses this named variant and decides it fits the collection’s Hindu-story scope.** It must not be spliced with the familiar Chittor/Rāṇā plot or relabeled as that plot. For the current canon hook, the inspected Mīrā-attributed poem still supports the cup and devotional singing but not “she sings first”; Priyādās’s exact Chittor scene still needs direct edition-level inspection. No current-canon 3/6-minute draft is cleared yet.
