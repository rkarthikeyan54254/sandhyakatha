## Native-language package: Pārvatī

Both locale files map every narration scene to one or more of the six English source claims, have native titles and canonical identity markers, and keep separate short/full texts. Hindi and Tamil are independently drafted, not approved. Their machine schema/source-map checks are useful preparation only. The Hindi draft's substantially longer whitespace-token count than the Tamil draft is not itself a timing verdict; Tamil word boundaries differ, and neither has a human timed reading. The required next review is of the **rendered** Hindi and Tamil pages by fluent human readers, including natural title and spoken phrasing, source fidelity against chapters 26–28, and actual short/full seconds. Only after those reviews should `languageEditor` and `nativeReadAloud` be recorded as approved and the locales bound to final English bytes.

## Art suitability

The four 1448×1086 WebP files in `art/` are candidates with `humanVisualApproval: pending` and no production media selection. The Pārvatī card depicts an aged staff-and-umbrella visitor, Pārvatī and her friend, which fits the selected scene; a reviewer should assess whether the animal-skin garment on Pārvatī implies an unsupported characterization. The Lakṣmaṇa card shows a circular ground boundary and an apparent ascetic, but the image should be checked at its final crop for **exactly seven** legible lines and for the absence of a victim-blaming cue. The Kaṇṇappar card shows a hunter with the shrine and a priest; its foreground bananas/fruit may soften or confuse the source's deliberate meat-offering contrast, so it needs an editorial visual review. The Dakṣa card presents a tense sacrifice confrontation and avoids depicting Satī entering the ritual fire; its use still needs the 12+ disclosure and a parent visual review. None of these observations substitutes for human image approval or an approved OG/share rendition.
