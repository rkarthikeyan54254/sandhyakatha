## `janabai-grinding` — revise the hook before narration

The [Marathi abhaṅga collection with original-language panels](https://www.anantasatsang.org/janabai/abhangas.html) has separate witness clusters for Viṭṭhal helping Janābāī grind and the accusation over his missing pendant. The latter is serious: Janābāī's own first-person verse tells of a search, the pendant found in her sari, physical assault, a crowd, ropes, and an execution stake beside the Candrabhāgā. The stake becomes water. A [study of the life tradition](https://www.anantasatsang.org/janabai/life.html) also distinguishes the poems from Mahīpati's later narrative. Do not turn this into a harmless household accusation, and do not present the grinding and pendant scenes as one uninterrupted incident without a checked textual bridge. The canon hook says “her necklace goes missing”; the missing ornament is **Viṭṭhal's**. The grinding stone “turns faster than it should” is not the claim in the located verse; the deity comes to work beside her. Fix those claims in the canon first. A parent care note must state the accusation, beating and threatened impalement if the full theft episode is used; `minAge: 6`, `sensitivity: [social-hierarchy]` needs reassessment. A safer bounded telling may use only the grinding verses and place the theft as a distinct, parent-facing note, but then the present hook/title/close must be rewritten accordingly.

## `bhasmasura-hand` — current source attribution is not established

The familiar Bhasmāsura/Mohinī dancing sequence cannot presently be attributed to the canon row's “Śiva Purāṇa” locus. The [Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 10.88](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/88/) has the structurally related **Vṛka/Vṛkāsura** account: Śiva grants a boon that kills the person whose *head* Vṛka touches, Vṛka pursues him, and Viṣṇu comes as a **brahmacārin**, persuading Vṛka to test the boon on his own head. No Mohinī dance there. A [Purāṇic reference entry](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/bhasmasura) identifies the Bhasmāsura story in the Marathi *Śivalīlāmṛta* and explicitly says the demon is not mentioned in the Sanskrit Purāṇas; this is a secondary claim that should be checked against the actual Marathi passage before retelling. Current hook also overstates the boon as “whatever he puts his hand on” when the related Sanskrit account specifies a person's head. Either research and cite the *Śivalīlāmṛta* witness for a regional Bhasmāsura telling, or revise the row to Vṛkāsura/Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.88 and tell that distinct story. Do not blend the two or mark this row source-grounded yet.

## `boy-insults-shiva` — grounded, but the title and value need care

The [Śiva Purāṇa, Rudra-saṃhitā, Pārvatī-khaṇḍa 26–28](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226091.html) directly supports Śiva appearing as a brahmacārin, questioning Pārvatī's tapas, and describing the ash, matted hair, serpents, skull, bull and lack of conventional household standing. [Chapter 28](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226092.html) has Pārvatī defend Śiva, refuse to hear more disparagement, prepare to leave, then see the visitor reveal himself as Śiva. The source does not reduce her response to “holding your ground politely”: it includes anger and severe language about disparagement. The canon row's “young student” is a fair presentation of brahmacārin, but “quite accurately, everything wrong with the man” editorially endorses the visitor's hostile appraisal of Śiva and conflicts with the repository's rule against narrator belittlement. Revise the hook to make the challenge and disguise clear without endorsing those judgments. Keep the brahmacārin's critique attributed to the visitor; let Pārvatī's reply carry the other view. Do not imply that he is an unrelated boy after the reveal. The passage can support separate short/full renditions after a claim map and age review; no AI-authored Hindi/Tamil edition could satisfy the human language-editor gate.

## `sekkizhar-first-word` — prior hold remains correct

The earlier [source note](../../2026-10-03-second-five/source-notes/sekkizhar-first-word.md) already identified the unsupported “for days” in the canon hook. [Shaivam's history of Sēkkiḻār](https://www.shaivam.org/to-practise/the-history-of-sekkizhar/) supports the king's request, prayer at Chidambaram and the word *ulakelām*. The [opening verse of the Periya Purāṇam](https://shaivam.org/to-practise/life-of-the-sixty-three-nayanmar-periyapuraanam/) verifies that word in the poem, but the divine-word episode is a later life tradition, not Sēkkiḻār narrating his own composition. The [invocation commentary](https://www.shaivam.org/devotees/invocation-paayiram-of-periya-puranam/) explicitly treats the celestial-word report as a tradition behind the verse. Revise the hook to remove a multi-day inability and any invented failed attempts. Use `regional` with a tradition note naming the later hagiographic layer. A full rendition needs substantial sourced material beyond the single opening-word event; do not pad with imagined days or conversation merely to reach six minutes.
